<h4><b>Davide Casarin</b></h4>
<h6><i>Research Fellow @ Ca’ Foscari University of Venice</i></h6>

<p>
Davide Casarin is a <b>Computer Scientist</b> who obtained his <b>B.Sc. in Computer Science, Data Science curriculum</b> from <i>Ca’ Foscari University of Venice</i> in July 2026. In September 2026, he will begin an <b>M.Sc. in Mathematics – Computational Mathematics and Modelling</b> at the <i>University of Trieste</i>.
</p>

<p>
He is currently a <b>Research Collaborator</b> at the <i>Department of Environmental Sciences, Informatics and Statistics</i> at <i>Ca’ Foscari University of Venice</i>, within the <b>FRACTALS Research Group</b>. His research lies at the intersection of <b>computational mathematics, continuous non-linear optimization, and global sensitivity analysis</b>, with a focus on the <b>automatic parameterization and calibration of computationally expensive physical simulators</b>.
</p>

<p>
His research activities have contributed to <b>two co-authored abstracts accepted at international scientific venues</b>, including the <i>COSPAR Scientific Assembly</i> and the <i>European Space Weather Week</i>. His work combines mathematical and computational methods to develop efficient approaches for the analysis, calibration, and optimization of complex physical models.
</p>

<p>
In parallel with his academic and research activities, he has accumulated <b>four years of professional experience as a Software Engineer</b>, working across <b>full-stack development, Android applications, and industrial logistics systems</b>. His technical experience includes <b>C#, Python, Java, and RPG ILE on IBM i</b>, as well as performance and reliability refactoring of production services and the development of internal tooling for <b>static code and log analysis</b>.
</p>

<p>
His interests lie at the intersection of <b>computational mathematics, optimization, scientific computing, and software engineering</b>, with a particular focus on translating mathematically grounded methods into <i>efficient, reliable, and scalable computational systems</i>.
</p>
